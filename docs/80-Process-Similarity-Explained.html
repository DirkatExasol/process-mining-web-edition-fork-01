How Process Similarity is calculated

The A/B Comparison badge condenses “are these two processes behaving alike?” into one number, Q(T₁, T₂) ∈ [0, 1]. This guide walks through the exact formula the app uses — and three fully worked bookstore examples, verified against the implementation.

Q(T₁, T₂) = 0.4·Q_var + 0.4·Q_nodes + 0.2·Q_cov. Three components, one weighted average: Q_var checks that both graphs explain the same journey variants equally well, Q_nodes checks that the same scored (business-critical) steps are reached, and Q_cov measures how much traffic fits both process models perfectly. Everything is computed from the two panels' filtered journeys — the badge compares what you see, not the whole project.
1

Where the score appears

In A/B Comparison the app loads two filtered views of a process side by side — A-Chart and B-Chart. A floating badge between the panels shows Q(T₁, T₂), a single number from 0 to 1 answering: how behaviourally alike are these two processes? It recomputes automatically whenever a filter or date slider changes on either side.

The badge is coloured by thresholds: green ≥ 0.70 (very similar) · blue 0.31–0.69 (partial overlap) · red ≤ 0.30 (largely different).

2

The inputs: variants, edges, scores

The calculation uses three things, all taken from the two filtered views (never the whole project):

  • Journey variants of each side — every distinct step sequence (e.g. Login → Browse → Basket → Checkout) with the number of journeys that took it. Both sides' variants are pooled; each variant's frequency is its journey count divided by the combined total.
  • Edge sets of both graphs — every transition arrow drawn in A-Chart (edges_A) and in B-Chart (edges_B).
  • Step scores — the optional SCORE values configured on steps. For each scored step the absolute value is used (a −10 failure step is as important as a +10 success step), taking the larger value if the two sides disagree.
Variants with a single step are skipped — they contain no transition, so there is nothing to align.
3

The building block: alignment cost

For every variant v and each graph T, the app computes how badly the variant fits that graph — the share of its consecutive step pairs that do not exist as an edge of the graph:

costT(v)  =  missing edges  /  edges in the variant
Basket → ✓ Redeem Coupon → ✗ Checkout → ✓ Order Confirmed

Checking a coupon variant against the B-graph (which has no coupon step): 1 of 3 edges is missing → costB = 1/3 ≈ 0.33. Against the A-graph all edges exist → costA = 0 (a perfect replay).

Why not formal alignments? This is a linear-time approximation of process-mining conformance alignment. It needs no solver, so the badge can refresh instantly on every filter change.
4

Component 1 — Q_var: symmetric explanation (weight 40 %)

Q_var asks: do the two graphs explain each variant equally well? Agreement means the costs match — both replay a variant perfectly, or both fail it to the same degree. What is punished is asymmetry: one side explaining a variant that the other cannot.

Q_var  =  1 − Σ freq · |cost_A − cost_B|Σ freq · max(cost_A, cost_B)

Variants that fit both graphs perfectly contribute to neither sum — Q_var is decided only by the imperfectly fitting variants. If nothing misfits at all, Q_var = 1.

Sharp in practice. Each side's variants always replay perfectly on the graph mined from the same journeys (cost = 0 on the home side). Any cross-side miss is therefore fully asymmetric — so Q_var behaves as a strict structural test: 1.0 when every variant fits both graphs, 0.0 as soon as any variant uses an edge the other side lacks. That is why the badge reacts decisively when a new path appears on one side only.
5

Component 2 — Q_nodes: do the important steps agree? (weight 40 %)

Q_nodes compares which scored steps are effectively visited under each graph. A step's visit is “softened” by how well the variant fits: visit = (step in variant) × (1 − cost). Each step is weighted by |score|, so high-value (and high-damage) steps dominate; unscored steps do not participate at all.

Q_nodes  =  Σ freq · Σsteps min(visit_A, visit_B) · |score|Σ freq · Σsteps max(visit_A, visit_B) · |score|

A Jaccard-style min/max ratio: 1.0 when both graphs reach the same scored steps with the same confidence, lower the more the “important” steps differ. With no scored steps anywhere, Q_nodes defaults to 1 (it can neither help nor hurt).

6

Component 3 — Q_cov: what fits both perfectly? (weight 20 %)

Q_cov is the simplest of the three: the share of all journey frequency whose variant replays with zero missing edges on both graphs simultaneously.

Q_cov  =  Σ freq · [cost_A = 0 and cost_B = 0]

If 90 % of journeys follow routes that exist unchanged in both charts, Q_cov = 0.90 — regardless of how the remaining 10 % misfit.

7

Putting it together

The final score is a fixed weighted average — structure and scored-step agreement carry equal weight, joint coverage is a secondary signal:

Q(T₁, T₂)  =  0.4 · Q_var  +  0.4 · Q_nodes  +  0.2 · Q_cov
≥ 0.70 → green · very similar 0.31 – 0.69 → blue · partial overlap ≤ 0.30 → red · largely different

Worked example 1 — identical twins → Q = 1.00

Both panels show the same bookstore segment: 90 % of journeys take the main route, 10 % redeem a coupon on the way. Every variant exists on both sides, every edge is in both graphs.

A-Chart: EU orders — 5 % redeem a coupon. B-Chart: NA orders — 5 % add gift wrap. The shared main route carries 90 % of all journeys. Scored steps: Checkout = +5, Order Confirmed = +10.

Structurally strict (Q_var 0: each side has a route the other lacks), yet the score lands mid-range because the valuable steps and 90 % of the traffic behave identically. Verified against ab_similarity: 0.5640.

Reading it. A blue mid-range badge with healthy volume typically means: same core process, side-specific extras. Filter each side down to its exclusive variants to see exactly what differs.

Same date range, but A-Chart is filtered to confirmed orders and B-Chart to orders with a failed payment. Scored steps: Checkout +5, Order Confirmed +10, Payment Failed −10, Refund Processed −5 (absolute values are used).

The red badge confirms these are behaviourally different populations — which is exactly what the comparison was set up to prove. Verified against ab_similarity: 0.2923.

Good to know

  • No badge is better than a wrong badge. The score is not computed when either side has no variants, and it is hidden when the two sides together exceed 1 000 distinct variants (500 per side) — a score from a biased sample would mislead.
  • Scores shape the comparison. Q_nodes only sees steps with a SCORE value. Scoring your critical steps (positive or negative) makes the similarity metric focus on what matters to the business rather than treating all steps alike.
  • It compares the filtered views, not the projects. Change a filter and you change the question being asked — the badge always answers for exactly the journeys on screen.

Rule of thumb: green — treat the two views as the same process; blue — same core with real side-specific behaviour worth inspecting; red — different behaviours, compare with care.
