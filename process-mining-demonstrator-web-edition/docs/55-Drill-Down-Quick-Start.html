Aggregates & Drill-Down

Collapse a busy part of a process into one Σ super-step so the map stays readable — then drill down to the real steps behind it, and drill back up. This guide covers both: building aggregates (developers) and drilling into them (everyone).

An aggregate is only a way of seeing the process — it never changes the data. A developer collapses a connected set of steps into a Σ super-step (steps 1–4); from then on anyone can open that Σ step to inspect the sub-process it stands for — in a separate panel or expanded right inside the map (steps 5–9). Every figure matches the original flowchart for the same date range.
1

What an aggregate is — and who builds one

An aggregate collapses a connected set of steps into a single Σ super-step, so a busy map reads at a glance. Building one is a developer task; it produces a high-level project (the map carrying the Σ step) plus a detail project that keeps the real sub-process for drilling. The originals are never touched.

Security→ Passport→Boarding
⇒ Σ Security & Boarding Σ

Three connected steps become one Σ super-step on the high-level map — the detail is kept for drill-down.

Numbers stay honest. The Σ step’s counts and times are computed from the real events (a run of member-steps becomes one Σ event), so the high-level map and the detail always tell the same story.
2

Enter aggregate mode

Open a process map as a developer. In the lower-left corner of the canvas, click Σ Select steps to aggregate to switch the map into pick mode — plain clicks now select steps instead of opening menus.

Only developers and admins see this button. Click it again (or Esc) to leave pick mode without creating anything.

3

Pick a connected set of steps

Click the steps you want to collapse. They must be interconnected — each reachable from another in the set. Picked steps are highlighted, and an action bar shows the count.

Σ Aggregate 3 steps selected ＋ Add group Create map (1)

Highlighted = picked. Click a step again to deselect.

  • Several aggregates at once. Click ＋ Add group to bank the current group and start another — a step can belong to only one aggregate.
  • Already on a high-level map? The button reads Add to map, adding more Σ steps to the map you have open.
Connected only. If a picked step isn’t linked to the rest, the app flags it — an aggregate has to be one contiguous sub-process.

Spot a Σ aggregate step

On the high-level map, aggregated sub-processes appear as a single accent-coloured step whose name starts with Σ. In the left Projects list, aggregate maps are marked with a Σ too.

7

“New panel” — the sub-process on its own

Choose Drill down · new panel to open the aggregate’s steps in a pop-up panel, wired to the steps just before and after it. The panel shows:

Dashed steps (Check-in, Gate) are the incoming / outgoing connections — shown for context, kept outside the aggregate. The chips switch the edge metric, just like the main map.

  • The step’s incoming and outgoing connections (with their metrics) are drawn.
  • The date range and journey count are shown at the top.
  • Switch the metric with the chips (Count, %, Avg/Min/Max Time, Std Dev).
  • The panel is resizable — drag its bottom-right corner; the chart re-fits.
Tip. The panel opens fitted to the whole sub-process. Close it with ✕ or Esc to return to the map.
8

“In place” — expand inside the map, and Drill up

Choose Drill down · in place to burst the Σ step open within the current map: its real steps replace the Σ node and connect to the same neighbours, while everything else stays exactly where it was. To collapse it again, use ⤴ Drill up.

Check-in→ Security→ Passport→ Boarding→ Gate

The Σ node is gone — its steps are now part of the map, in its place. ⤴ Drill up (above the map, or any step’s menu) collapses them back into Σ.

  • Surrounding steps and their group boxes don’t move; only the expanded part changes.
  • Other Σ steps stay collapsed — you can expand several of them independently.
  • ⤴ Drill up re-collapses the exploded steps back into their Σ super-step.
Which one? Use in place to keep the surrounding context while you look inside; use new panel to focus on just the sub-process.
9

Numbers follow your filters

The drill-down always reflects your current date window — it’s shown in the panel — and updates automatically when you move the date slider or apply a filter. So a Σ step’s detail and the high-level map always tell the same story.

Why totals can look higher inside. An aggregate only hides steps for readability; the detail counts the same journeys as the high-level map for the same dates. (Journey-duration, score and step-count filters aren’t applied to the detail, because a collapsed map measures those differently than the full data.)

Good to know

  • Aggregates vs groups. A group (coloured box) just visually clusters steps on a map; an aggregate collapses connected steps into a drillable Σ super-step. The new Σ step inherits the members’ group, so grouping and aggregating stay consistent.
  • Drill down is read-only. You’re inspecting the real process — nothing is changed.
  • Show or hide aggregates. Under Configuration → Aggregations you can hide the aggregate detail projects/connections from the sidebar to keep it tidy; the high-level map always stays visible.
  • Who does what. Creating Σ super-steps is a developer task; anyone who can open the map can drill into them.

Aggregates make a large process readable at a glance, and drill-down lets you go from that overview straight to the real detail — without ever leaving the numbers behind.
